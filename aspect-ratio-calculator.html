How cropping for print works

To fill the whole sheet, the printer scales your photo until both sides of the paper are covered, then trims the overflow. So a shape mismatch is always paid from the longer side relative to the print: a wide photo printed squarer loses its left and right edges, and a squarer photo printed wide loses top and bottom. Two practical consequences:

  • Keep subjects away from the edges that will be trimmed — for a 3:2 photo going to 8×10, that is 16.7% of the long side in total.
  • Cropping also spends resolution. The trimmed pixels no longer count toward the print, which is why the size pages on this site report effective PPI after the crop. You can check the pixel budget for each size below.
