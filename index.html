How the calculation works

Print size is pixels divided by resolution: print inches = pixels ÷ PPI. PPI (pixels per inch) describes how densely pixels are spread across the paper. More pixels per inch means finer detail:

  • 300 PPI — Excellent Sharp at close viewing distance. The standard for photos you will hold or inspect up close.
  • 200 PPI — Good Hard to tell apart from 300 PPI at normal viewing distance. A practical target for most enlargements.
  • 150 PPI — Acceptable Fine for wall prints viewed from a few feet away, such as posters and large canvas prints.

Example: a 6000 × 4000 pixel photo (24 megapixels) prints at 20 × 13.33 inches at 300 PPI, 30 × 20 inches at 200 PPI, and 40 × 26.67 inches at 150 PPI.
