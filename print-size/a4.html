Pixels Needed for A4 and A3 Prints

A4 (210 × 297 mm) needs 2480 × 3508 pixels (about 8.7 megapixels) at 300 PPI. A3 (297 × 420 mm) is exactly twice the area, so it needs twice the pixels: 3508 × 4961 (about 17.4 MP).

A4 and A3 in inches

Printers and PPI work in inches, so first convert: A4 is 210 × 297 mm = 8.27 × 11.69 inches, and A3 is 297 × 420 mm = 11.69 × 16.54 inches (millimetres ÷ 25.4). Each side in inches × PPI gives the pixels needed.

Worked example: 210 mm ÷ 25.4 = 8.27 in, and 8.27 × 300 = 2480 px; 297 mm ÷ 25.4 = 11.69 in, and 11.69 × 300 = 3508 px. Together that is 2480 × 3508 = 8,699,840 pixels ≈ 8.7 megapixels.

Notice the pattern: each A size's short side is the next size down's long side — A3's short side is 297 mm, exactly A4's long side, which is why A3 at 300 PPI starts at 3508 px, the same figure as A4's long side at 300 PPI. Every A size shares the same shape — a 1:√2 ratio (about 1:1.414) — so an image cropped correctly for A4 fits A3, A2 and beyond with no further cropping, it just needs more pixels.

Good to know for A sizes

Neither cameras (3:2) nor phones (4:3) shoot in the A-series 1:√2 shape, so filling an A4 or A3 sheet always crops something — for a 3:2 photo the crop comes off the long side. Documents and artwork are usually viewed up close, so 300 PPI is the right target for A4 handouts; A3 posters on a wall are fine at 200 PPI. For US paper sizes instead, see the 8×10 and 11×14 pages — note that US Letter (8.5 × 11 in) is slightly wider and shorter than A4.
