How Large Can I Print a 6000×4000 Photo?

6000 × 4000 pixels is the classic 24 megapixel file from a DSLR or mirrorless camera. At top quality (300 PPI) it prints at 20 × 13.33 inches — and it stays above the 150 PPI poster threshold even at 24×36.

Largest print from 6000 × 4000, by quality level

ResolutionLargest print sizeQuality
300 PPI20 × 13.33 inExcellent — close viewing
200 PPI30 × 20 inGood — normal distance
150 PPI40 × 26.67 inAcceptable — wall viewing

The maths is pixels ÷ PPI for each side: 6000 ÷ 300 = 20 in and 4000 ÷ 300 = 13.33 in at the highest quality tier.

What this means in practice

  • 8×10 and 11×14: excellent, with resolution to spare (500 and about 364 PPI) — cropping for composition costs you nothing at these sizes.
  • 16×20: good at 250 PPI, comfortably above the 200 PPI wall-print target.
  • 24×36 poster: about 167 PPI — above the 150 PPI threshold for prints viewed from a few feet away, and 6000 × 4000 is exactly 3:2, the poster's own shape, so nothing is cropped.

These figures assume the full, uncropped file from the camera. Heavy cropping, digital zoom or a copy saved from a messaging app will have fewer pixels — check the actual file with the calculator above.
