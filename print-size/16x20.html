Pixels Needed for a 16×20 Print

For a sharp 16×20 inch print at 300 PPI you need 4800 × 6000 pixels (28.8 megapixels). At 200 PPI — usually indistinguishable on a wall — you need 3200 × 4000 pixels (12.8 MP).

Pixels needed for 16×20, by quality level

ResolutionPixels neededMegapixelsQuality
300 PPI4800 × 6000 px28.8 MPExcellent — close viewing
200 PPI3200 × 4000 px12.8 MPGood — normal distance
150 PPI2400 × 3000 px7.2 MPAcceptable — wall viewing

The maths is print inches × PPI for each side: 16 in × 300 = 4800 px and 20 in × 300 = 6000 px, and 4800 × 6000 = 28,800,000 pixels = 28.8 megapixels.

Crop and aspect ratio: 16×20 is 4:5

16×20 has a 4:5 aspect ratio (16 ÷ 20 = 4 ÷ 5), the same shape as an 8×10. That is squarer than most original photos:

  • A typical DSLR or mirrorless photo is 3:2 — printing it at 16×20 crops the long ends (for example, a 6000 × 4000 px file becomes 5000 × 4000 px after cropping to 4:5).
  • A typical phone photo is 4:3 — closer to 4:5, but it still needs a small crop.

Plan for the crop: check that heads, hands and text are not tight against the long edges of your frame before you order. The calculator above accounts for both orientations, but cropping to 4:5 can lower your effective resolution versus the uncropped file.
